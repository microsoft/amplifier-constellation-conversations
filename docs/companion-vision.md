# Companion vision

Companion brings a familiar agent into your physical world through live talk,
seeing what you choose to share, and a shared visual surface. We want conversation
to feel like working alongside someone: ask a question, point something out,
change direction, and keep talking while useful work happens in the background.

The first priority is **show and help**. Show an object you are assembling, compare
two arrangements, or work through something unfamiliar together. The agent should
connect what you show with what you are trying to accomplish, ask useful
questions, and help you take the next step. You should be able to stay engaged
with the thing in front of you without continually returning to a keyboard.

The shared visual surface should help explain a thought, remember a useful
detail, or compare alternatives. Treat it like a small whiteboard: show what
helps now, revise it as understanding changes, and put away what no longer needs
attention. Meanwhile, the agent may collaborate or carry work forward elsewhere.
Bring back the result or question that matters to this conversation, without
making the person listen to every intermediate exchange.

Begin with one person and one agent. The longer-term ambition is shared calls
where several people and agents can think and work together. Each agent should
remain a recognizable individual, with its own identity and voice, and everyone
should be able to understand who is contributing.

Companion is the product experience; Conversations supplies reusable conversation
services that can support it and other experiences. This vision leaves devices
and interaction design open. The [current call service and reference client](companion.md)
describe implemented support and its limits. The [shared agreements](README.md#shared-agreements)
define the commitments between the services behind these experiences.
